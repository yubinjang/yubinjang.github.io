Following federal dollars to the schools where they land.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Education and Social Policy at the University of Delaware. I study how policy intended to strengthen the educator workforce is translated, redirected, and sometimes lost between statute and classroom.

01 About me

I grew up in classrooms on two continents and kept asking the same question in both. When a government decides to invest in teachers and school leaders, what actually reaches them?

I came to the University of Delaware in 2023 after an M.A. in Education and Educational Administration and a B.A. in Education at Hanyang University in Seoul. My Korean work examined teacher collaboration, participatory school climate, and professional learning across OECD systems, using multilevel structural equation modeling to trace how school-level conditions shape what teachers are able to do.

My dissertation carries that question into American federalism. Title II, Part A is the federal government's principal investment in educator quality, yet the money moves through state formulas and district discretion before anyone teaches a class with it. I follow it the whole way, pairing national spending data with interviews and document analysis in Delaware districts, in partnership with the Delaware Department of Education.

I expect to complete my Ph.D. in May 2027 and I am on the 2026 to 2027 academic job market.

02 Research agenda

Three strands, one question. What happens to the investments and opportunities meant to strengthen teaching and leading, between the policy that creates them and the educators they are for?

i.

Federal funding and district decision-making on educators and leadership

Title II, Part A sends roughly two billion dollars a year to states for educator quality, with unusually wide local discretion over how it is spent. My dissertation pairs a national portrait of allocation and expenditure with a mixed-methods study of Delaware districts, asking how administrators decide, what constrains them, and where funds become fungible with other priorities.

  • Partners: Delaware Department of Education, CDDA
  • Published in Educational Policy (2026)
  • Presented at AEFP, SREE, UCEA, APPAM

ii.

Equity in the educator and leadership labor market

Who is hired, who is promoted, and who leaves. Using longitudinal National Association of Independent Schools data, Dr. Bailes and I test whether crisis conditions change the odds that women and leaders of color are hired into headships. Related work follows retention and turnover, including Delaware's teacher paid parental leave program and teacher quality in career and technical education. A companion scoping review examines the material metaphors the field uses to describe racialized and gendered career differences.

  • Partner: National Association of Independent Schools
  • Working paper with preprint, co-first authorship
  • Scoping review of racialized and gendered career metaphors
  • Presented at AERA, UCEA, NAIS Annual Conference

iii.

Professional learning and its effects on teaching and student outcomes

Professional development is the most common lever policy pulls and the least reliably effective. I lead the analysis on a Gates Foundation funded evaluation of a culturally responsive professional learning partnership across four districts, estimating both who gets access to it and what it changes for students. Related work with Mathematica looks at culturally responsive materials and teacher learning in middle school mathematics, and a parallel study asks what Delaware principals actually receive when they are sent to learn.

  • Funders: Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation ($9.1M and $1.5M), UD Research Foundation, Spencer
  • Collaborator: Mathematica
  • Manuscripts under review at Journal of Educational Change and International Journal of Leadership in Education
